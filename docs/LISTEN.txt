FORGE 9.3 — listening guide
===========================

All files are rendered by FORGE's own engine (the same code the app plays), 256 kb/s MP3. Levels are in LEVELS-*.txt.
Best on headphones or good speakers. Nothing here is a sample of another record or machine.

1. DRUMS — one groove, seven kits  (drums-*-v9.3.mp3)
   The same tech-house drop, the same notes, only the kit changes:
     drums-forge   the 9.2 synthesized voices (for comparison)
     drums-808     808-style: soft square-wave hats, tonal snare, long clap tail
     drums-909     909-style: bright 6-bit cymbals, punchy noise snare
     drums-707     707-style: crisp 8-bit µ-law snare and hats
     drums-linn    Linn-style: 28 kHz / 8-bit sampled character, round snare
     drums-acoustic  membrane modes, snare wires that rattle with the head, a small room
     drums-lofi    dark, saturated, 22 kHz tape-ish
   Listen for: the hats no longer sound identical hit to hit (three variants in rotation, softer hits darker);
   the open hat is choked by the next closed hat; the snare has body, wires and a room.

2. HARMONY — where the chords meet the kick  (harmony-*-v9.2.mp3 vs harmony-*-v9.3.mp3)
   Same genre, same seed, before and after:
     deep-house drop, melodic-techno drop, trance break, dnb drop, house groove
   In 9.2 every chord change lands on the downbeat with the kick. In 9.3 listen for changes that arrive a 16th or an 8th
   early (pushed, the house "anticipation"), or an 8th after the kick (the techno / dub entrance), and section starts that
   do not all hit on the one.

3. TRANSITIONS — the same two tracks, ten ways  (transition-*.mp3)
   house → tech house, each file = 2 bars before the mix, the mix, 2 bars after:
     blend      EQ blend, the bass swaps on a bar line in the middle
     filter     B opens from a low-pass while A climbs into a high-pass
     echo       A leaves into its own tempo echo
     stems      drums change hands at 1/4, bass at the middle, music at 3/4
     breakdown  B's music (no drums, no bass) over A's groove, then the rest of B on the line
     tease      two one-bar glimpses of B, then a blend
     cut        drop swap: B's first drop lands on the line, A's last beat thrown into the delay
     roll       A's last bar rolls 1/4 → 1/8 → 1/16 into the cut
     brake      A's tape stops on the last beat
     riser      a noise riser and a high-pass climb over A, B lands on the line

What to send back: which kit sounds closest to what you want per genre; any harmony excerpt where the push or the late
entry feels wrong; which transitions you would never use (Auto-DJ can be told to avoid them).
